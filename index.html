What the document is

It's an announcement, not a proof. Alpöge says so directly: the choice was to “only present Lean” rather than post a “massive unreadable travesty of ‘slop’” (the natural-language version runs over 250,000 pages). The technical timeline on pages 3–4 is described as a paraphrase of a Claude summary that “might make zero sense.” So the note gives you the shape of the idea, and the 14 tarballs are the actual proof.

The core idea

The Riemann hypothesis is already a theorem for curves over finite fields (Weil, 1940s). Stepanov later gave an elementary proof of it: build an auxiliary polynomial that vanishes to high multiplicity at every point you want to count. A polynomial can't have more zeros, counted with multiplicity, than its degree allows, so the count gets bounded.

Over the integers, multiplicity means taking derivatives, which would be “something like differentiating whole numbers.” That's a well-known reason function-field methods don't carry over.

The claimed way around it is to use theta lifts as stand-ins for multiplicity. Each lift in a chain between definite unitary groups, U(X₀) → U(X₁) → … → U(Xₘ), contributes one order of multiplicity. For the prime-counting sum up to X, Claude proposed a chain of length m ≈ √X. That's the line Alpöge says will make mathematicians “fall out of their chairs.” RH is itself equivalent to the prime-count error being about √X.

The positivity side goes through the Guinand–Weil explicit formula. Weil showed RH is equivalent to a certain functional W being nonnegative on a class of test functions. Several timeline items are inequalities of the form W(…) ≥ 0, proved with determinants.

Other things worth noticing

  • Timeline: about six weeks, from Oct 6 to “11/16/26: GRH.”
  • Breadth: Iwasawa theory, relative trace formulas, Gan–Gross–Prasad and Bessel periods, Chowla–Selberg, Siegel mass formulas, the Bost slope inequality, a weakened Vandiver conjecture, even Behrend's construction.
  • Scope: RH and GRH are claimed. The Extended Riemann Hypothesis is only expected “with more time.”

The strategy is coherent and specific, the kind of plan an expert would take seriously. But much of pages 3–4 is compressed shorthand I can't really follow, and nothing in the note lets anyone check correctness. The Lean is the only way to check it.

I found it, or at least something very close to it. One caveat up front: the quantity under the square root isn't literally the X from the note, so this is my best reading rather than a certainty.

The code

theorem GlobalTheory.T.log_kap_le_and_lenT_mul_le_sqrt : ... →
    Real.log (kap p i) ≤ 2 * L  ∧  (lenT p i PP : ℝ) * PP ≤ Real.sqrt b
def lenT (PP : ℝ) : ℕ := ⌈PP ^ 2 * dimFlag ^ 2 * k / m⌉₊      -- the chain length
def binT (PP : ℝ) : ℝ := ((lenT p i PP - 1).choose dimFlag)    -- a counting term
def zed  (PP : ℝ) (u1) : ℝ := 7 * (dimFlag ^ 2 / lenT p i PP + ...)  -- error shrinks as lenT grows

The idea in plain terms

The basic fact. A polynomial of degree 10 can cross zero at most 10 times. If you force it to touch zero at a point instead of crossing (multiplicity 2), each point uses up two of its budget, so it can only do that at 5 points. With multiplicity m, the limit is 10/m points.

Stepanov's trick. To show there aren't too many of something, build a polynomial that vanishes with multiplicity m at every one of them. Then (number of things) × m ≤ degree. Larger m gives a better bound, but building such a polynomial gets harder as m grows; the choose term in binT counts that cost. So there's a sweet spot.

The obstacle. Multiplicity normally comes from derivatives, and you can't take the slope of the number 7.

The new idea. Each theta lift in a chain acts like one more derivative, so a chain of m lifts gives multiplicity m. lenT is the chain length.

Why √X. Each lift helps, but the cost grows faster. The theorem caps chain length × PP at √b. Making the chain as long as the budget allows lands on a square root, which matches the size RH is about.

What I'm unsure about

Under the square root is b, which the code defines as exp(xε), not literally the X from the note. My guess is that the prime sums at this stage have length b, but I traced only one thread through 175,000 files.

Claude

Yes, that's the core of it, with one adjustment.

What you have right is the “budget” logic. A polynomial has a fixed budget of zeros, and making it vanish more strongly at each point uses that budget up faster.

The adjustment is about what gets counted. The method doesn't count zeta's zeros directly:

  1. You build a helper function that vanishes, with high multiplicity, at the things you want to count.
  2. The zero budget says there can't be “too many” of them.
  3. That bounds how irregular the primes can be: the counting error is at most about √X.
  4. A classical equivalence says the primes are that regular if and only if all of zeta's zeros lie on the line Re = ½.

Your picture of inputs curling around into complex outputs fits zeta itself well. The zeros are where that output passes exactly through 0, and RH says that only happens along one vertical line.

Measured which areas of mathematics dominate the source, and counted famous named results

1. Number fields and function fields: from analogy to bridge

For about 80 years, mathematicians have known the integers resemble polynomials over finite fields; Weil called this his “Rosetta stone.” RH was proved on the polynomial side in the 1940s, but the techniques never carried over. If the proof holds up, theta lifts play the role of derivatives and the bridge is real.

2. Most of the effort is geometry

  • 57% of the files (99,000) are about abelian varieties.
  • The biggest section is DivisorProofs, with 42,000 files: the Stepanov-style divisor, the zero-budget object itself.
  • Multiplicity is measured by divisibility by high powers of a prime p, which is where heights, CM curves and Arakelov geometry come in.

3. The Langlands program is everywhere

“Langlands” appears in 4,355 files, with whole sections on Howe duality, the Rallis inner product, local Langlands and Gan–Gross–Prasad periods.

4. Some pieces are just strange

  • Behrend's construction from additive combinatorics, which Alpöge calls “obviously going to be unnecessary.”
  • The exceptional group G₂ appears in about 2,000 files.
  • Side results: a weakened Vandiver conjecture and vanishing of some K-groups of ℤ, though “Vandiver” doesn't appear by name in the code.

5. A correction

I had guessed the 7,000-file WeilBound section was a new proof of the Weil and Deligne bounds. It's actually about the Weil representation, the engine behind theta lifts.

What it means for the primes

Thousands of papers beginning “Assuming GRH…” would become unconditional. Primes in any arithmetic progression appear by about (q log q)². Gaps between consecutive primes near p are at most about √p · log p. The Miller primality test becomes provably correct and fast.

The meta-surprise

A complete, machine-checked proof exists before anyone, including its author, fully understands it. That has never happened with a result this important.

i have a question about the constructing derivitives for integers: does this in any way have anything to do with calculus in fractional dimensions? i know this doesn't exist yet

Claude

Not directly. But your instinct points at real things, and one of them is a known reformulation of RH.

First, calculus in fractional settings partly exists already: fractional calculus (half-derivatives) goes back to Leibniz and Riemann, and analysis on fractals has been developed since the 1980s.

Why this proof's “derivative” is a different idea

The derivative needed here is about multiplicity, how strongly something vanishes. Over the integers the proof measures it by divisibility by primes and by theta lifts, each of which earns one more whole order of multiplicity. Nothing fractional is going on.

Where your instinct does connect

  • Derivatives of integers are a research direction. Buium's p-derivation, roughly δ(x) = (x − xᵖ)/p, behaves like a derivative, and Borger related it to the “field with one element.” Alpöge's footnote 5 mentions that road.
  • Fractional dimensions touch RH. Lapidus and Maier proved RH is equivalent to a statement about vibrating fractal strings. This proof just doesn't use it.
